It has been about 5 weeks now, so my details would be a bit fuzzy. Funny how the human mind forgets.

It was just another Saturday of playing futsal with my friends. It was an unusual day: I was going alone, when normally I would have the companionship of my elder brothers. One had gone overseas for a work related trip while another was off to Islamabad for a competition. It felt weird.

Anyways, it's football. If you'd knew me well, you would know that I would never miss an opportunity to play football. I filled my 4L Stanley (yes you read that right: no one actually bothers to bring a water bottle apart from a select few), grabbed the football, and scooted off for the ground.

My brothers weren't playing today, so I knew I was going to start. In a 5v5, I preferred to play at the back, since I am better suited to spot the runs, not make them. The game started a bit haphazardly. I believe positioning and chemistry are the most important things on a field; that day, both were way off. Passing lanes were left uncovered, two of us were pressing one player with the ball leaving a player empty and potentially in a perfect position to score: it was a mess. I wondered how the play would have been had my my elder brothers been there: one was a calming presence, slow but proactive in anticipating the attackers' movements and defusing them, while the other was a perfect blend of chaos; he could weave around the opponents in one snappy motion as if a gust of wind flew past your back, but for the love of God could not finish a 1v1. I hate to admit it, but I missed them that day.

And then it happened.

I was pressing the defender in unsettled possession, when he put a shoulder feint to throw me off. It was nothing out of the ordinary, just a simple shoulder to the side to counter my press. I shifted, pivoting on my left foot to twist my body towards my goal. Unfortunately, my left foot stuck to the turf and I was stuck in a rock and a hard place: Either my ankle was going to bear the brunt of the twist, or my knee. My ankle locked at that moment, making the decision for me. I broke down with a cracking sound from knee, the sound reminiscent of a bone breaking. Except that it was not the bone. It was the ligament in the knee, the main support we call the __ACL__. I writhed in pain while I clutched my knee, as if I had lost a key component of my body. Two of my teammates supported me off the turf as I hobbled on my right leg, with my left foot dragging on the turf, unable to bear any of my weight. In the time since my injury, I have seen 2 players from PSG, Khvicha Kvaratskelia and Desire Doue, suffer the same fate as me. It was a common pattern: A fleeting moment in a normal play, a load in an awkward position and boom: ACL torn.
 
ACL tear is renowned in the football community as one of the most common yet one of the most debilatating injury in the sports. Even the most optimistic of the timelines continue rehab for 6 months. __6 months__. 6 months without football, and knowing myself, it could well be over that.

It was a fleeting moment, but it seemed as if it was a long time: Within that twist, I thought about my shoes, how they were a size bigger than the tight fit I normally wear, and how my toe was hitting the turf whenever I took a shot. My toe had stuck on the pitch. Perhaps it was the shoe: perhaps a divine intervention, to provide me an opportunity. An opportunity to reflect.
